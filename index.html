About the lab

Engineering matter at the nanoscale

CNL brings together materials synthesis, mechanism-driven experiments, modelling and interface science, connecting molecular mechanisms to macroscopic behaviour.

Our work combines experiments and computation in equal measure. In the laboratory, we synthesise nanoparticles, engineer colloids and interfaces, and develop drug-delivery systems and catalytic materials. In parallel, we build population balance models, Monte Carlo simulations, thermodynamic models, molecular dynamics and docking studies. Each side drives the other: experiments test our models, and models guide our next experiments.

Research

From synthesis to application

Magnetic and metal-oxide nanoparticles, heterostructured catalysts, polymer-stabilised nanocarriers and computational drug design.

Theme 01

Nanoparticle formation, morphology control and growth models

How nanomaterials nucleate, grow and change shape in thermal decomposition, coprecipitation and solvent-less routes: prenucleation clusters, metastable intermediates, thermodynamic modelling and Monte Carlo simulation of aggregation.

Theme 02

Drug delivery, cancer therapy and biological interfaces

Polymer-coated, shape-controlled magnetic nanoparticles for targeted therapy: cellular uptake, membrane deformation energies, PEG-dependent stability and drug–nanocarrier synergy.

Theme 03

Catalysis and energy nanomaterials

Pd/magnetite heterostructures, metal oxides and nano-hybrids for electro-oxidation, hydrogen generation, CO₂ capture and battery thermal management, through facet engineering.

Theme 04

Colloids, interfaces and nanoparticle–membrane interactions

Aggregation physics, polymer-mediated stability and Monte Carlo models of dispersion state and membrane engulfment of nanoparticles.

Theme 05

Computational chemistry for drug discovery

Docking, molecular dynamics and in-silico screening: estrogen receptor-α inhibitors, SARS-CoV-2 receptor mimics and tirzepatide analogues.

Integrating experiments, mechanistic modelling and molecular simulation to design nanostructures with tunable morphologies.

Prof. C. Ravikumar’s research spans nanotechnology, materials science, and chemical engineering, with a focus on understanding and engineering the formation, assembly, interaction, and functionality of nanoparticles. His group integrates experiments, mechanistic modelling, molecular simulations, and computational chemistry to design nanostructures with tunable morphologies for applications in catalysis, therapeutic delivery, energy systems, and interfacial science.

He received his PhD in Chemical Engineering from IIT Bombay, where he investigated nanoparticle formation mechanisms in aqueous and organic systems. He later undertook postdoctoral research at the New Jersey Institute of Technology (USA) on top-down approaches for producing stable drug nanoparticles.

Read full biography

Prof. Ravikumar has made significant contributions to the understanding of nanoparticle nucleation, growth, surface modification, and solvent-mediated morphology control, with publications in leading journals such as Journal of Physical Chemistry C, Langmuir, Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research, Crystal Growth & Design, Journal of Molecular Liquids, and Materialia. He also holds Indian patents on the controlled synthesis of iron oxide nanoparticles.

His research excellence has been recognised through multiple awards, including the IIT Bombay Best PhD Thesis Award, Battelle Best Employee Award, and several Best Oral/Poster Presentation Awards. His students have received national recognition for work in nanoparticle-based drug delivery and cancer therapeutics.

Prof. Ravikumar has led sponsored research projects funded by SERB, UGC-DAE-CSR, defence agencies, and industry partners, covering areas such as catalytic nanomaterials, hydrogen production, targeted cancer nanocarriers, environmental remediation, and biocompatible materials.

At IIT Dharwad, he serves as Associate Dean (General Outreach) and actively contributes to institutional planning, academic development, and outreach. He has also organised international conferences, advanced nanotechnology workshops, and hands-on training programs, while remaining deeply engaged in scientific and societal outreach.

Open research topics

PhD projects open to upcoming research scholars. Each combines hands-on experiments with modelling.

Topic 01

Magnetic nanocarriers for targeted drug delivery

Design polymer-coated, shape-controlled iron oxide nanoparticles that carry anticancer drugs and can be steered to a tumour with an external magnet. The project covers synthesis, surface functionalisation, drug loading and release, and cellular uptake.

Topic 02

Magneto-dielectric ferrite composites for compact antennas

Develop ferrite-based composites whose magnetic permeability matches their permittivity, so antennas can be made smaller without losing bandwidth. Grain size, composition and filler loading will be tuned to keep losses low at microwave frequencies.

Topic 03

Perovskite oxygen carriers for chemical looping

Engineer ABO₃ perovskites that give up and take back lattice oxygen in repeated cycles, enabling fuel conversion and hydrogen or syngas production with built-in CO₂ separation. Focus on doping for oxygen capacity, fast redox kinetics and long-term stability.

Topic 04

Population balance and colloidal modelling of nanoparticle formation

Build population balance, Monte Carlo and colloidal-interaction models that predict how nucleation, growth and aggregation set the final size and shape of nanoparticles. Models are tested against the lab’s own synthesis experiments.

Topic 05

Nanomaterials for CO₂ capture and conversion

Create porous and catalytic nanomaterials that capture CO₂ selectively and convert it into useful fuels and chemicals such as CO and methanol. The work links adsorption capacity and active sites to conversion efficiency.
